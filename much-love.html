Much Love

Redesigning the Much Love Animal Rescue website to make adoption, fostering, volunteering, and donating feel clear and inviting — transforming an overwhelming wall of information into a guided, step-by-step experience.

Role UX Designer
Timeline 1 Month · 2021
Platform Web (Desktop + Mobile)
Tools Figma, Miro, HTML/CSS/JS

Overview

The Challenge

Much Love Animal Rescue's website had a fundamental information architecture problem: it contained a wealth of helpful content, but users couldn't find the most important things — how to adopt a pet, what the process involved, or how to donate effectively. Content was text-heavy, processes were buried, and donation options were so numerous they created decision paralysis.

The goal was to redesign the site so that anyone arriving with intent to adopt, foster, volunteer, or donate could immediately understand what to do next — guided step-by-step rather than left to hunt through dense pages.

Mobile redesign — clear calls to action and streamlined content hierarchy replacing the original text-heavy layout.

Research

Understanding the Users

Research combined a survey of ten respondents, five one-on-one interviews, a SWOT competitor analysis of three animal rescue organizations, and a heuristic evaluation of the existing Much Love site. The existing site's problems came into sharp focus:

  • No clear adoption process — users didn't know what steps to follow or what was expected of them.
  • Content was too text-heavy — important information was buried inside paragraphs instead of surfaced as scannable steps.
  • Too many donation options — abundance created confusion, not generosity, leading to abandonment.
  • Users wanted access to animal medical history upfront — a key trust signal that was missing entirely.

"Love animals but I don't know if I am ready to have my own."

— Primary persona, reflecting the anxiety around commitment that the design needed to reduce

Process

Designing the New Experience

The design process began with storyboarding and task flow mapping for the two most critical journeys: adoption and donation. These flows became the backbone of the redesign — every screen was evaluated against whether it moved the user confidently toward completing one of these goals.

  • Storyboard — mapped the emotional journey from first visit through adoption completion to set the right tone for each screen.
  • Task flows — diagrammed the adoption and donation processes separately before wireframing, identifying where the existing site forced unnecessary detours.
  • Style guide — developed a warm, visually appealing design language that conveyed care and trust before a single interaction.
  • A/B testing — four design variations were tested to validate the most effective layout for the adoption flow.

Solution

The Redesigned Site

The redesign replaced a content-dump homepage with clear calls to action and dedicated guided flows for each user intent. Rather than expecting visitors to read their way to a decision, the site now leads them through structured steps — reducing anxiety about the adoption process and making donations feel purposeful rather than overwhelming.

01

Guided Adoption Flow

A pop-up qualification step surfaces eligibility and fee information upfront — removing surprises late in the process that caused abandonment. Users are walked through each step sequentially rather than presented with a wall of requirements.

02

Animal Medical History

Research identified this as a top-requested feature — and a key trust signal. Displaying medical history on each animal's profile page reduces hesitancy and helps prospective adopters make more confident decisions.

03

Streamlined Donations

Reduced the donation experience from a confusing multi-option page to a clean amount selector with one clear confirmation step. Fewer choices, faster completion, more confident donors.

04

Responsive Mobile Design

Full mobile-first redesign with responsive pop-ups and touch-friendly interaction patterns — meeting users where they are rather than assuming a desktop context.

Testing

A/B Testing & Iteration

Four design variations were tested to validate the most effective approach for the adoption flow. The primary question was how to present the qualification information — pop-up, slide-in panel, or inline form — to maximize completion without creating friction. Three iterations refined the winning pattern.

Reflection

What I Learned

This project demonstrated how much damage a misaligned information architecture can do to a mission-driven organization. Much Love had the content — they just presented it in a way that overwhelmed rather than guided. The most impactful change wasn't a visual redesign; it was reordering information so the right thing appeared at the right moment in the user's decision process.

The A/B testing phase was particularly instructive. All four variations felt "reasonable" from a design perspective, but user testing revealed that seemingly small differences — where the qualification pop-up appeared, how fees were disclosed — had significant effects on users' confidence and willingness to continue. Design intuition isn't enough: you have to test.

If continuing this project, I'd add a live chat or quick-contact feature for prospective adopters with specific questions — the research showed that uncertainty about the process was the biggest barrier, and a human touchpoint at the right moment could meaningfully improve conversion.
