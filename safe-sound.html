Safe & Sound

A drive-in music festival experience designed for the pandemic era — combining safety measures, mobile ticketing, drone food delivery, and social features so attendees could enjoy live music without compromising their health.

Role UX Designer
Timeline 1 Month · 2021
Platform Mobile App + Website
Tools Figma, Miro, HTML/CSS/JS

Overview

The Challenge

By 2021, people were deeply fatigued by COVID restrictions and had exhausted their at-home entertainment options. They wanted to go out — to feel the energy of a live event — but remained genuinely concerned about their health. Existing event formats couldn't reconcile these two needs.

The challenge was to design a drive-in music festival experience with a companion app and website that made safety measures feel enabling, not limiting. The product needed to convince hesitant attendees that they could have a genuinely good time while staying safe.

Mobile app map view — designated parking areas with distance spacing and nearby attendee discovery.

Research

Understanding the Audience

The research phase combined a proto-persona, online surveys, and six one-on-one interviews with users matching the target demographic — primarily adults aged 22–30 who were socially active before the pandemic. Key findings:

  • Over 33% of respondents said they missed fun activities more than anything else during COVID restrictions.
  • 48% wanted events held inside vehicles or fenced areas to maintain clear physical separation between groups.
  • 67% preferred to view ticket information on desktop or laptop before purchasing.
  • Vaccination incentives — fast-lane entry, discounts — were seen as fair and motivating, not coercive.

"People are generally tired of COVID restrictions and have exhausted all of their at-home entertainment options. While they are still very concerned about their health and safety, they are searching for a safe, engaging, smoothly run event."

— Research synthesis from surveys and user interviews

Process

From Idea to Prototype

The ideation phase used "I like, I wish, what if" brainstorming followed by a prioritization matrix to narrow the feature set. A clear design language emerged: electric and exciting with retro nightlife elements, reinforcing that safety measures enhance rather than diminish the festival vibe.

  • Brainstorming — generated a wide feature set around parking, food, social interaction, and safety screening.
  • Prioritization — mapped features against feasibility and user value to identify the MVP for prototyping.
  • Style guide — defined the visual language before wireframing to keep the high-energy aesthetic consistent.
  • Storyboard & task flows — mapped the full attendee journey from ticket purchase through food ordering and social interaction at the event.

Solution

The Final Design

Safe & Sound ships as a mobile app for in-event use and a desktop website for pre-event ticket browsing and purchasing. The two products cover the complete attendee journey from discovery to departure.

01

QR E-Tickets

Contactless QR code tickets include vaccination record verification. Vaccinated attendees access a fast lane, turning a safety measure into a perk that encourages health compliance.

02

Venue Map & Parking

An interactive map shows designated parking zones with enforced spacing between groups. Attendees can see nearby groups on the map and initiate contact — social connection without physical crowding.

03

Drone Food Delivery

Food and drinks are ordered in-app and delivered by drone directly to each vehicle — eliminating queues and contact points that represented the highest safety risk at live events.

04

Group Chat & Sharing

Attendees can message nearby groups, share food orders, and coordinate meetups through the in-app chat — maintaining the social energy of a festival within a safely distanced environment.

05

Desktop Ticketing

The desktop website serves as the primary pre-event destination — event details, ticket tiers, FAQ, and directions — matching the 67% of users who preferred desktop for ticket research.

06

Coded Prototype

Key flows were built as a partial coded prototype in HTML/CSS/JS — enabling more realistic usability testing than a static Figma prototype, particularly for animation and micro-interaction feedback.

Testing

Iteration & Refinement

Usability testing uncovered five specific changes that improved clarity and reduced friction across the food ordering, social, and check-in flows. Each iteration was driven by a concrete user observation, not assumption.

Reflection

What I Learned

This project reinforced that constraints can be features. Every COVID safety requirement — vehicle parking, contactless tickets, drone delivery — became a design opportunity to differentiate the experience. Reframing restrictions as novelties shifted the product narrative from "a concert you have to attend carefully" to "a new kind of festival."

The coded prototype was worth the extra time. Animating the ticket QR code and food ordering flow in real HTML revealed transition issues that a Figma prototype would have hidden — and gave testing participants a much more authentic reaction to the final product.

If I continued this project, I'd explore a live news feed of festival posts to encourage organic socializing between nearby groups, and reach out to existing drive-in event operators to validate the operational feasibility of the drone delivery concept.
